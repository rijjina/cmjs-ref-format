## I. GENERAL EVALUATION & OVERVIEW
The manuscript investigates the fatigue response and transformation behavior of annealed vs. chemical-etched TiNiCu helical shape memory alloy (SMA) springs under cyclic displacement at 80 °C. The topic is relevant to low-temperature waste heat engines and thermomechanical actuators. 

However, the manuscript suffers from unsupported core claims, severe statistical under-sampling, direct internal contradictions between text and data, and an alarming rate of corrupted/hallucinated DOIs and citations stemming from unverified Generative AI post-processing.

---

## II. MAJOR SCIENTIFIC CRITIQUES & OVERCLAIMING
1. **Unsubstantiated Determination of 'Endurance Limit' (N = 1):** The authors test only three extension ratios. At 1.825 extension, both etched and non-etched survive 10^6 cycles, with only N=1 tested per condition due to 'time constraints'. Claiming a higher endurance limit based on N=1 runout is scientifically invalid.
2. **Direct Contradiction Between Text and Experimental Data:** In Section 3.1, etched springs survived nearly double the cycles of non-etched springs (90k vs 50k at 3.000x, 25k vs 12k at 3.625x). Yet Paragraph 77 asserts etching 'significantly reduced the fatigue life'.
3. **Confounding of Wire Dissolution vs. Oxide Film Removal:** 1:4:5 HF:HNO3:H2O vigorously dissolves Ti and Ni. For a 0.7 mm wire, a 4% diameter loss drops torsional stiffness by 16% (k ~ d^4). Wire diameter was never measured post-etching.
4. **Thermodynamic Incoherence in DSC:** Narrow thermal hysteresis is desirable in waste heat engines. Claiming it represents 'diminished shape memory effect' contradicts both the literature and the authors' own introduction.
5. **Missing Metrology:** Confocal 3D microscopy is claimed in Section 2.4, but zero Sa, Sq, or Sz values are presented.
